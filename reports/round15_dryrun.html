Round 15 dry run and D133 proposal

Round 15 dry run (development seeds only) and the D133 registration proposal

Status: dry run complete; awaiting the owner’s approval before any formal seed is used. Records: reports/round15/dev/*.jsonl; summary reports/round15/dev_summary.json; code committed locally. Development seeds 961–966 (verified unused), 914 (the D123 AFCM development seed) and 22 (the CK-6c seed, a known failure case, used only to see whether a variant changes it). No formal seed (24; 3500–3519; 3600–3619; 3700–3719) has been used. Every dry-run number below is a single development replicate: it shows mechanics and direction, not evidence.

1. What the dry run found

  1. symbolic_distill works as intended.
    • The unconstrained refit again destroyed the learned shapes (identifiable RISE 0.197 → 1.781 on S-Add, 0.503 → 5.184 on S-Sparse), even though no restricted-domain formula was involved on these seeds.
    • The distillation refit kept them (0.200 and 0.502) and slightly improved the validation partial likelihood on S-Add (4.2151 vs 4.2205 after pruning; S-Sparse 4.2479 vs 4.2477), with IBS unchanged.
    • Validation chose μ = 1, the largest value in your grid, on both seeds; smaller μ let the shapes drift (μ = 0.01: 0.593 and 0.675).
    • The domain guard was never triggered on these two seeds (no log, sqrt, 1/x or exp formula was accepted), so its safety property is shown only by the unit tests (R15-1: finite for any parameters; no change on the training range).
  2. b2s_res_cox as specified (residual curve X(t) − X_last) is misspecified when the whole trajectory matters.
    • On S-Sparse (truth ∫sin(πX/2)dt) it was worse than plain B2-S Cox (curve error 0.0924 vs 0.0828): a nonlinear effect of X(t) cannot be written as an effect of X_last plus an effect of X(t) − X_last.
    • On S-Last (truth depends on X(1) only) the gate correctly switched every functional channel off, and the model beat B2-S Cox (0.1727 vs 0.1800), close to the static last-value CoxKAN (0.1679).
  3. The anchored variant fixes this. Same last-value edges and the same validation gate, but the gated B2-S term acts on the full curve X(t) instead of the residual, so the model nests both B2-S Cox and the pure landmark model.
    • S-Sparse: 0.0810 (better than B2-S Cox 0.0828 and the residual design 0.0924).
    • S-Last: 0.1736 (gate off; like the residual design).
    • On the small, aids-shaped synthetic set the gate switched the functional part off and both gated variants lost to B2-S Cox (IBS 0.1985/0.1993 vs 0.1946): with about 80 validation subjects the gate is conservative.
  4. afcm_ens does not resolve the CK-6c sensitivity.
    • On seed 22 (CK-6c’s failure) every variant still had φ₁ ≈ 0.24: the 3-resample bag 0.239, per-channel λ_t 0.240 (and φ₂ rose to 0.108), the D120 parsimonious λ rule 0.247. All bootstrap resamples chose λ_t = 0.01: validation genuinely prefers the time-varying fit on these data, so averaging over resamples cannot change it.
    • On seed 914 the variants were worse than the single V3 fit (V3 0.023; bag 0.056; parsimonious 0.092; per-channel λ_t 0.224). On seed 966 V3 0.055; bag 0.097.
    • V3 itself passed the CK-6c criterion on 2 of these 3 seeds, so a single fresh seed (24) is close to a coin toss whichever variant is used.

3. D133: the registration I propose (needs your approval)

Fresh seeds (verified unused in every code file, test and record on 2026-10-24): CK-6d 24; S-Add 3500–3519; S-Sparse 3600–3619 (3–8 visits); S-Last 3700–3719 (3–8 visits). Seeds 25 and 26 are also unused and are not assigned. DGP v2, n 1,000 (CK-6d: n 2,000), split_groups 60/20/20 on δ, B-spline front end, the D112 ensembles, as in Round 12. Every run is reported, failures included.

Family A: symbolic_distill (as you specified). Per S-Add and S-Sparse replicate: the Round-12 pruning procedure on the B2-S Cox seed-0 model, stages 1–2 (PySR is not needed: its formulas are never substituted), then the domain-guarded substitution and the distillation refit with μ ∈ {1e-2, 1e-1, 1} chosen by unpenalised validation NLPL. Also recorded: the no-refit model and the old unconstrained refit.

  • H-R15a (confirmatory; both parts must hold):
    1. all 40 distillation refits finite;
    2. on S-Add, distilled RISE minus RISE after pruning (mean identifiable RISE, paired over 20 replicates): the upper 95% t-bound below +0.05 (non-inferiority; the H-R12 margin).
  • Secondary (exploratory): the same on S-Sparse; distilled minus old refit; distilled minus no-refit in IBS, curve error and validation NLPL; how often μ = 1 (the grid edge) is chosen; how often the guard is active.

Family B: landmark-anchored functional CoxKAN. On S-Last and S-Sparse (the same replicates as family A): B2-S Cox, static CoxKAN on last values + z, landmark Cox, the residual design (your specification) and the anchored variant.

  • I recommend the anchored variant as the primary model of H-R15b, with the residual design as a registered secondary. If you prefer to keep the residual design as primary, the dry run predicts that H-R15b part 2 below fails.
  • H-R15b (confirmatory; both parts must hold):
    1. on S-Last, primary minus B2-S Cox in survival-curve error: the 95% CI entirely below 0;
    2. on S-Sparse, the same difference: the upper 95% bound below +0.005 (non-inferiority; the P0 margin).
  • Secondary: the same contrasts against static CoxKAN and landmark Cox; the residual design; the gate’s decisions, including whether it removes the null channel.
  • Real data (exploratory only, not prospective). PBC E2/E1, prothro and aids on the Round-13 splits 0–4 (so the saved Round-13 records of B2-S Cox, static CoxKAN and landmark Cox are the paired comparators, after a deterministic reproduction check on one split); Nadeau–Bengio intervals; the last-value edges checked against the pre-stated clinical directions. The design was motivated by Round 13 on these same data, so nothing here can be confirmatory.

Family C: AFCM. The dry run predicts that afcm_ens does not fix the single-fit sensitivity. I need your choice:

  • C1 (as you specified): CK-6d on seed 24 with afcm_ens: the CK-6c procedure and criterion (identifiable RISE φ₁ < 0.15 and φ₂ < 0.10). If it passes, H-R15c (conditional confirmatory) re-runs the H-R12 comparison against afcm_ens on S-Add 3500–3519 with the H-R12 rule; if it fails, AFCM stays “not validated”.
  • C2 (my recommendation): do not spend seed 24 now. Implement REML/Laplace-type smoothing selection (the method of mgcv, which the original AFCM uses) on development seeds first. The dry run shows that validation-NLPL selection itself, not variance, causes the failure.
  • C3: replace the single-seed acceptance check by a multi-seed one (for example, at least 4 of 5 fresh seeds pass), because a single fit passes or fails almost by chance (V3 passed 2 of 3 development seeds).

Budget (estimated from the dry-run timings). About 25 lane-hours (4 parallel lanes, about 7 h wall clock in this container).

4. Further ways to improve the model (my suggestions, not implemented)

  1. Shrink B2-S towards a time-constant effect.
    • In Round 12 the time-homogeneous B2-H had the lowest shape error (0.123 vs 0.179 for B2-S).
    • A roughness penalty on the time profiles h(t), chosen by validation, would let B2-S fall back to B2-H when the data do not support time variation.
    • This is the most promising change for shape recovery.
  2. Monotone edges in the pre-stated clinical directions. These would remove sign flips in small cohorts (the prothrombin-index misalignment) at little cost; the directions are already registered.
  3. Bootstrap bagging of the KAN ensembles instead of seed-only ensembles. CK-5b passed only through ensembling, so resample variance matters.
  4. A gate that is less conservative on small data: a cross-validated gate instead of one validation split (56–80 subjects on the real data).
  5. REML/Laplace smoothing selection for AFCM (option C2).

These are not part of D133; each would need its own development run and registration.
