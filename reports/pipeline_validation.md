# Pipeline validation (synthetic controls; about the METHOD, not about Bitcoin)

Generated by `scripts/pipeline_controls.py`. Each run creates a 240-day synthetic 1-minute price path and pushes it through the **same code** used for the real data: features, walk-forward logistic regression (C = 0.1), a long/short signal, and a backtest at base costs of 7 bp per side. The evaluation covers about 6 months of hourly decisions.

## What this shows

* **Negative control (no signal exists):** the mean AUC is 0.4982 and the mean gross Sharpe is -0.16, so the pipeline has **no systematic bias towards finding an edge**, which a look-ahead bug would create. The share of null runs whose AUC confidence interval wrongly excludes 0.5 is **5.0%** against a nominal 5%. That is close to nominal.
* **Single-run noise:** across random walks the gross Sharpe has a standard deviation of 1.14 over about half a year. One backtest showing a Sharpe of 1–2 is therefore **entirely consistent with zero skill**. This is the strongest argument for out-of-sample discipline and the Deflated Sharpe Ratio.
* **Costs:** with no edge, trading every hour loses money in 100% of runs at 14 bp round trip. Turnover alone destroys a strategy that has no edge.
* **Positive control (a real signal exists, beta = 0.3):** mean AUC 0.6234; the interval excludes 0.5 in 100% of runs. The pipeline **detects a genuine signal of this size**. (Beta = 0.3 is a strong effect, chosen to test detection rather than to resemble BTC.)
